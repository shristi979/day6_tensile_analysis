1. Young's modulus (stress-band linear fit, R² above 0.9997 in all 30 tests) averaged 69.9 GPa for aluminium, 117.2 GPa for copper and 199.0 GPa for steel, in line with typical handbook values; steel scatters most (±4.3 GPa), mainly because test_28 (189.1 GPa) and test_15 (194.6 GPa) stayed low in every fitting band tried, so they were kept, not removed.
2. Mean 0.2% offset yield strength was 354 MPa for steel, 244 MPa for aluminium and 152 MPa for copper; steel varied from 343 to 370 MPa across its 10 tests (standard deviation about 9 MPa).
3. Mean ultimate tensile strength was 484 MPa for steel, 289 MPa for aluminium and 222 MPa for copper; the UTS/yield ratio is about 1.46 for copper, 1.37 for steel and 1.18 for aluminium, so copper gains the most strength after yielding and aluminium the least.
4. Mean elongation (strain at the end of the usable record) was 31.4% for copper, 22.5% for steel and 11.5% for aluminium, but test_02 (4.6%), test_03 (15.3%) and test_26 (11.9%) end far below the rest of their material groups and pull these means down, so their fracture points should be checked before the elongation values are relied on.
5. All 30 tests were analysed with no failures and none were excluded; the moduli match handbook values, which gives no sign of a large machine-compliance effect, although displacement was not checked against an extensometer.
